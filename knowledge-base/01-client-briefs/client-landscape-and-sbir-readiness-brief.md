# Freight Trust Infrastructure

## Short client brief

Freight decisions are made with fragmented information about carrier identity,
credentials, facility events, and responsibility. That creates verification friction,
detention disputes, and uncertainty for brokers, carriers, shippers, facilities, and
insurers.

The opportunity is a **federated freight-trust layer**: a governed system that links
authoritative evidence and permissioned operating events without requiring participants to
pool raw commercial data.

### What it should prove first

- Carrier identity can be resolved more accurately and explainably.
- Facility events can be recorded with usable provenance.
- Participants will share limited data when they receive clear reciprocal value.
- Small carriers are not burdened by new manual entry, paywalls, or opaque scores.
- Corrections, appeals, abstentions, and human review work in practice.

The first pilot should focus on one workflow, one carrier cohort, one broker or shipper
cohort, and one or two facilities. Start with onboarding or a facility-event dispute.
Treat fraud reduction, detention reduction, and empty-mile reduction as measurable outcomes,
not assumptions.

### Why the timing matters

Detention is a material industry problem, FMCSA is modernizing identity and anti-fraud
processes, and the Supreme Court's *Montgomery* decision has increased the importance of
broker evidence without creating a universal duty-of-care standard.

### Immediate decisions

1. Select the first buyer and workflow.
2. Recruit pilot partners and one permissioned event source.
3. Approve the benchmark and experiment plan.
4. Confirm the NSF SBIR/STTR applicant, PI, and submission route.
