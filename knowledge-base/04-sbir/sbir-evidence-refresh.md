**Additional context found, not directly asked for but material to planning:** SBIR/STTR authorization nationally lapsed 2025-09-30 through 2026-04-13 — the longest lapse in the programs' 42-year history, during which NSF (and 10 other agencies) could not open new Phase I/II competitions. The *Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act of 2026* (S. 3971) reauthorized the programs through 2031-09-30; Project Pitches reopened 2026-06-02. This explains why the guide's planning horizon starts where it does, and corroborates rather than contradicts its dates. Confidence: secondary (multiple trade outlets — Granted AI, Government Contracts Legal Forum — converge on the same dates); worth a primary confirmation via congress.gov for the bill number/date if this history is ever cited directly in a proposal.

## Market and problem evidence

**1. Cargo theft losses, 2025 (CargoNet/Verisk).** Total reported/confirmed cargo theft losses reached an estimated **$725 million in 2025**, up **60% year-over-year**; confirmed incident count rose 18% (2,243 → 2,646) while total supply-chain-crime reports were roughly flat (3,607 → 3,594); average value per theft rose 36% to $273,990. Source: Verisk/CargoNet analysis, covered by [Work Truck Online](https://www.worktruckonline.com/news/cargo-theft-losses-surged-to-an-estimated-725-million-in-2025) and [Carrier Management](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2026/01/22/283728.htm), reported ~2026-01-22. Confidence: **secondary** — CargoNet is a recognized industry data aggregator (a Verisk product), reasonably neutral on facts but commercially motivated as a security-services vendor; CCJ's own coverage returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch and could not be independently cross-checked this pass.

**2. Broader "organized cargo theft" — $35B/year figure.** Multiple 2025–2026 trade outlets (Inbound Logistics, Tank Transport, Talk Business & Politics, ATA) repeat a figure of **up to $35 billion annually** in U.S. cargo theft losses, attributed to Homeland Security Investigations, with theft "up 1,500% since 2021." Confidence: **unverified** — this figure is widely repeated in secondary press but I could not locate a citable primary HSI document publishing it directly in this session; it appears to be a persistently-recirculated statistic. **Do not cite as a hard HSI-sourced number without locating the original HSI publication or testimony.**

**3. TIA "State of Fraud in the Industry," April 2025 (2nd edition).** Member-survey findings: 83% of respondent brokerages experienced at least three distinct fraud types within six months; 22% lost more than $200,000 to fraud in the prior six months (10% spent >$200,000 on prevention); 34% cited "unlawful brokerage" (impersonating a legitimate broker) as the top fraud tactic; TIA's Watchdog platform logged 1,600+ fraud reports Sept 2024–Feb 2025, a 65% increase over the prior period. Source: [TIA news release, 2025-04-28](https://news.tianet.org/tia-releases-state-of-fraud-in-the-industry-april-2025-report/). Confidence: **secondary** — TIA is an industry trade association surveying its own broker membership (not neutral academic research), but it is the most direct member-level fraud-incidence data available and is the standard industry-cited benchmark.

**4. Double-brokering cost estimate, ~$700M–$1B/year.** Several 2025–2026 secondary/aggregator sources attribute an estimate of **$700 million to $1 billion annually** in double-brokering-related losses to TIA. Confidence: **unverified/attributed** — I could not fetch a TIA-published document stating this specific range directly; it appears only in secondary aggregation. Treat as directionally indicative pending a primary TIA citation.

**5. ATRI "An Analysis of the Operational Costs of Trucking: 2025 Update" (published ~July 2025, reporting on 2024 data).** Non-fuel marginal operating cost reached **$1.779/mile (+3.6% YoY)**, described as the highest ever recorded by ATRI; total marginal operating cost approximately $2.26/mile for 2024. Average unproductive dwell time per stop reported as **~1h38m**, down 2 minutes YoY, per FreightWaves' coverage. Source: [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/atri-report-rising-costs-continue-to-squeeze-trucking-industry), [Cottingham & Butler](https://www.cottinghambutler.com/post/2025-trucking-operational-costs-and-industry-trends-key-takeaways-from-atri-s-latest-report) (2025-10-13). Confidence: **secondary** — the original ATRI PDF sits behind a lead-generation form and could not be fetched directly this session; figures are as reported by trade press summarizing it. **Flag: an unrelated web search separately returned a "1h49m" 2025 dwell-time figure from a different secondary source — this conflicts with the 1h38m figure above and could not be reconciled this pass. Confirm against the primary ATRI PDF before using either number in a proposal.**

**6. Detention economic-loss estimate — $15.1B/year.** Widely repeated figure: detention causes **$15.1 billion/year** in economic loss ($11.5B lost productivity + $3.6B added expense), equating to **$11,000–$19,000 lost per driver per year**; collection-rate figures vary by source (29.3% per one citation, "fewer than 50%" per another). Sources: [ABL Trucking](https://www.abltrucking.com/post/the-real-cost-of-driver-detention-what-the-data-tells-us) (2025-06-13), [Optym](https://www.optym.com/blog/the-cost-of-detention-time-in-trucking). Confidence: **unverified/mixed** — this figure is persistently cited across secondary trucking-industry blogs but appears to trace to an **older ATRI detention study (circa 2019–2020)**, not confirmed as restated in the 2025 Operational Costs Update specifically. **Do not attribute this figure to "ATRI 2025" without confirming it appears in the current-year report** — it may be a stale number being recirculated as if current.

**7. FMCSA Motus registration-system rollout.** Federal Register doc. 2026-08334 (published 2026-04-29) confirms Phase I of Motus launched 2025-12-08 (limited to insurers/financial-responsibility filers/transportation service providers); full availability to all regulated entities went live by mid-May 2026 (2026-05-14 per trade coverage), retiring legacy systems (Unified Registration System, FMCSA Portal, MCMIS). No new contradicting information found for the July–August 2026 window beyond what evidence.md's §G2 already documents (paused deactivations, login/verification friction). Confidence: **primary** for rollout mechanics (Federal Register); **secondary** for the "rocky rollout" characterization (unchanged from evidence.md). Source: [Federal Register 2026-08334](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/29/2026-08334/availability-of-motus-fmcsas-new-registration-system).

**8. Freight-forwarding/onboarding-compliance software market size.** One paid market-research firm (Business Research Insights) sizes the "Freight Forwarding Management Software" market at **~$0.67B in 2026, growing to ~$1.76B by 2035 (11.3% CAGR)**. Confidence: **unverified/vendor market-research** — methodology not disclosed in available snippets; this is a broad software category, not a dedicated market-size figure for carrier identity-verification or compliance-verification tools specifically. **No neutral, dedicated market-size figure for the identity-verification/compliance-verification sub-segment was found this pass** — this remains a gap for the commercial-potential section.

**9. Carrier-onboarding abandonment / synthetic-identity fraud figures.** A vendor-oriented identity-verification trade blog (finsmes.com, 2026-07) cites a **34% onboarding-abandonment rate** and states deepfake/synthetic-identity fraud accounts for **~22% of digital identity fraud attempts** (2026). Confidence: **unverified/vendor-claimed** — sourced from an identity-verification-platform-adjacent blog rather than a neutral study; do not cite without locating the underlying primary study (likely an Entrust or Regula identity-fraud report) and confirming it is freight-specific rather than general digital-identity fraud.

## Corrections needed

No claim in the Readiness Guide was directly contradicted by this research pass — every checked figure (deadlines, award cap, duration, Project Pitch fields/limits, PI rule, SBIR/STTR budget splits, registrations, cost-sharing prohibition) matched the current nsf.gov / seedfund.nsf.gov pages on direct fetch. The following are additions/refinements for the guide's owner to fold in by hand, not corrections of factual errors:

1. **TABA is not mentioned in the guide at all.** Add: TABA is available up to **$6,500 for Phase I**, and under the current mechanism it must be budgeted **inside** the $305,000 base proposal rather than requested as a separate post-award supplement (contrast with the older two-step process described in NSF's archived `nsf20-070` guidance, which the guide's own linked sources may not distinguish from the current rule).
2. **"Invitation valid for two deadlines" could not be re-confirmed against current primary-source text this session** (see table above). Not contradicted — just unconfirmed. Recommend a manual visit to the live Project Pitch / full-proposal instructions before this specific mechanic is relied on for scheduling.
3. **The guide doesn't mention the 2025–2026 SBIR/STTR reauthorization lapse** (2025-09-30 to 2026-04-13) or the reopening of Project Pitches on 2026-06-02. This is background, not an error, but it materially explains *why* the guide's planning horizon starts where it does and would strengthen the "why 4 March 2027 is the base case" reasoning if cited.
4. **The dwell-time and detention-cost figures pulled for this refresh (see market-evidence items 5 and 6) contain an internal discrepancy** (1h38m vs. 1h49m) and a possible stale-figure risk (the $15.1B detention-loss number may be older than "ATRI 2025"). This is a caution for whoever drafts the Problem and Significance section next, not a guide correction: verify against the primary ATRI PDF rather than trade-press paraphrase before citing either number.
