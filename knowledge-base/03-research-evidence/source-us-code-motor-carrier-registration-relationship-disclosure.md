## What these statutes establish for E1

### The registered object is a person

Section 13902 directs the Secretary to register a **person** as a motor carrier, subject to statutory conditions, including issuance of a USDOT number under § 31134. Section 31134 likewise states that an employer or person subject to the safety jurisdiction may operate only if registered and receives a USDOT number.

### Relationships are disclosed as relationships, not collapsed into identity

Section 13902 requires disclosure of relationships involving common ownership, common management, common control, or common familial relationship between the applicant person and another motor carrier, freight forwarder, broker, or applicant within the prior three years.

Section 31134 similarly distinguishes an employer/person seeking registration from other persons related by common ownership, management, control, or family relationship.

Section 31135 prohibits two or more motor carriers, employers, or persons from using those relationships to avoid compliance or conceal noncompliance/history.

**Core ontology implication:** federal law itself speaks in terms of multiple persons connected by relationships. Common ownership/control/familial ties are not evidence that the persons are legally identical.

## E1 consequences

- Gold-standard identity is anchored to the legally cognizable/registered person, not to a loose economic group.
- Ownership/control/family relations are graph edges between persons.
- The benchmark must permit multiple simultaneously active, legitimately distinct carriers under common ownership/control.
- Relationship evidence may be relevant to a separate continuity or regulatory-risk task but cannot define legal-person equivalence by itself.
