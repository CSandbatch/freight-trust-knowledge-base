## Negative finding — confirmed absence

**NPTC has no published position on any topic in this programme's scope.** Across all
twenty issues in its own May 2026 policy-positions document, NPTC states **no** position on:

- freight fraud, double brokering, or hostage loads
- motor-carrier identity theft, chameleon / reincarnated carriers, or registration fraud
- broker transparency (49 CFR 371) or broker duty of care
- cargo theft
- driver detention or dwell time
- carrier vetting, verification, or qualification data

The word "fraud" appears exactly once in the document, and in an unrelated context — the
Entry-Level Driver Training entry, noting "A DOT audit found significant non-compliance and
outright fraud in issuing training credentials," where NPTC's stated action is only that it
"confirmed that drivers whose CDL was issued in reliance on training certification from a
provider who was later delisted still have valid CDLs."

**Corroborating scan:** NPTC's press-release index (2023 through May 2026) contains no
release on fraud, carrier verification, broker transparency, cargo theft, or detention. All
releases in that period are awards, board elections, and certification announcements.

**Search scope for this absence, 2026-08-07:** NPTC's own policy-positions PDF (complete,
read in full); NPTC press-release index; nptc.org homepage navigation; web search
`"National Private Truck Council NPTC position statement freight fraud carrier verification
detention"` and `nptc.org "government affairs" OR "advocacy" private fleet comments FMCSA
rulemaking`.

## Limits and scope

- The absence is confirmed **as of the document's own date, 2026-05-01**. NPTC could take a
  position later; the document is a snapshot.
- NPTC's *Washington Report* teleconferences and *Regulatory Updates* feed are member-facing
  and were not accessed. A position could in principle be communicated to members without
  appearing in the public positions document. The public record shows none.
- NPTC's constituency is **private fleets** — companies hauling their own goods. Freight
  fraud and broker transparency are structurally less relevant to a private fleet than to a
  for-hire carrier, which is a plausible explanation for the absence but is *not* something
  the source states.

## Consequence for the programme

NPTC should not appear in a stakeholder matrix as an aligned or interested party on the
basis of remit. It is a documented non-participant in this policy space. If NPTC is wanted
as a pilot partner, the approach is a **facility-side / shipper-side detention and
dwell-measurement** argument — private fleets operate the docks — not a fraud argument.
