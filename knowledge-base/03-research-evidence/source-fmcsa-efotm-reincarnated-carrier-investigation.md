# FMCSA eFOTM v9.8 — operational investigation of suspected reincarnation/affiliation

## Citation

Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration, *Consolidated Electronic Field Operations Training Manual (eFOTM), version 9.8*, dated 2025-08-28.

<https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/2025-09/Consolidated%20Electronic%20Field%20Operations%20Training%20Manual%20%28eFOTM%29%20version%209.8.pdf>

Direct retrieval succeeded 2026-08-08 (18.1 MB PDF); local text extraction was used to inspect the reincarnated/affiliated-carrier sections.

## What the manual establishes

The manual describes common **first indications** of a potential reincarnated/affiliated carrier — such as an address used by another carrier, same phone numbers, or overlapping owners/officers/operators — but instructs investigators to focus on operational control and the § 386.73(c) standard.

When potential continuity is observed, the manual directs investigators to determine the suspected predecessor's FMCSA operational status, coordinate with Division Administrators and counsel, gather evidence about both predecessor and new entity, and build a chronology. It treats the screening tool as a wide-net lead generator and warns that common resources can be normal in some industry segments.

A chameleon/affiliate investigation is not treated as complete simply because the screening tool found similarities; the manual requires evidence collection, review, and an enforcement decision process.

## Consequences for E1

- Candidate generation and adjudication must be separate stages.
- Shared fields are **lead signals**, not identity labels.
- Reviewer packets should include a chronology rather than a timeless flat row.
- The standard should log unavailable evidence, not silently treat missing evidence as negative evidence.
- E1's automatic system must be evaluated as a triage/resolution aid, not as a substitute for FMCSA's legal determination process.
