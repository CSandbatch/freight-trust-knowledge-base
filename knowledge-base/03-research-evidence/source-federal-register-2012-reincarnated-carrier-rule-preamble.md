# FMCSA 2012 final-rule preamble — why common factors do not equal reincarnation

## Citation

Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration, final-rule preamble adopting the reincarnated-carrier / affiliate proceeding now codified at 49 CFR § 386.73, published 2012.

Public-inspection PDF: <https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2012-10162.pdf>

Current rule: <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-386/subpart-F/section-386.73>

## What the preamble adds beyond the rule text

The preamble is especially important for E1 because commenters raised the exact false-link problem that a graph matcher can create: separate fleets can have common ownership and can engage in many of the activities described in the continuity factors while remaining separate entities.

FMCSA's response was not to make common ownership, shared equipment, addresses, or any other single factor dispositive. The Agency stated that legitimate business changes are not affected merely because factors overlap; determinations rest on all relevant information, and one factor or source of evidence is not necessarily more significant than another. The Agency also retained purpose/intent in the standard and described its decision as a totality-of-the-evidence inquiry.

## Consequences for E1

1. **No single weak field is identity-dispositive.** Shared address, phone, owner, equipment, employees, insurer, customers, or name can generate a candidate or relationship hypothesis; none alone proves legal identity.
2. **Common ownership is not sameness.** The ontology must support simultaneous distinct legal persons under common ownership/control.
3. **Legitimate business changes must remain representable.** Merger, acquisition, asset transfer, restructuring, and ordinary common-control structures cannot be flattened into a chameleon label.
4. **Motive is a regulatory-disposition factor, not an identity label.** Identity gold-standard construction is blinded to safety/enforcement motive except when a separate regulatory-disposition label is being adjudicated.
5. **Evidence weighting must be empirically learned or rule-scoped.** E1 cannot claim FMCSA itself assigns a fixed universal weight to the 13 factors.
