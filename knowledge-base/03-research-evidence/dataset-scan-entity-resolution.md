# Dataset Scan — Entity Resolution Benchmark (Aim 1 / G14)

**Purpose.** Supports NSF SBIR Phase I Aim 1 (`04-sbir/drafts/phase-1-project-description-draft.md`,
Section 3) and goal G14 (`03-research-evidence/goals.md`, "Build the freight evidence benchmark"). Aim 1's every
numeric target is currently a bracketed placeholder because no qualifying adjudicated benchmark was identified in
the programme's documented evidence search. This scan looks for real, accessible seed data and
tooling from which the project could build an adjudicated carrier legal-person identity benchmark;
synthetic records are limited to controlled observation-noise challenges and cannot manufacture
authoritative reincarnation labels. No dataset, licence, or capability is inferred beyond its
source. Historical retrieval failures remain noted, but any superseding confirmed source card—not
a search-result snippet—controls current load-bearing claims.

## Chameleon-carrier / registration-fraud precedent

**GAO-12-364 ("Motor Carrier Safety: New Applicant Reviews Should Expand to Identify Freight Carriers Evading Detection," March 22, 2012).** Directly retrieved and confirmed via WebFetch of gao.gov. GAO's own operational definition of "chameleon attributes": a carrier (1) submitted registration information matching a previously registered carrier, **and** (2) that previously registered carrier had a motive for evading detection (e.g., a history of safety violations). GAO reported carriers with chameleon attributes rose from 759 (2005) to 1,136 (2010), and that 18% of chameleon-attribute applicants were involved in severe crashes vs. 6% of other new applicants (a 3x differential — this matches the "three times more likely" figure independently reported by Truck News/trade press). **GAO does not publish or release the underlying matched-record dataset or the specific fields used for matching** (ownership, address, phone-number attributes are referenced in secondary trade-press summaries of the report's recommendations, not confirmed as a field list in the primary GAO text itself on this pass — treat that specific field list as secondary-sourced, not primary). FMCSA's vetting program built in response covers only bus and household-goods carriers (2% of 2010 new applicants), explicitly excluding the freight-carrier population Aim 1 targets — this is itself evidence for, not against, Aim 1's premise that freight-carrier identity resolution is an underserved problem.

**FMCSA, "Implementation of Methodology to Identify Chameleon Carriers" (Report to Congress).** Exists and is publicly posted as a PDF at fmcsa.dot.gov, but the page returned HTTP 403 to automated WebFetch on this pass and could not be read directly — its content (methodology detail, any statistics, any released examples) is **not verified** and should not be cited beyond "this report exists and is publicly posted" until someone retrieves it directly (browser access, not automated fetch, may be required). Flagged as a follow-up, not resolved by this scan.

**DOT OIG enforcement precedent (not a dataset).** On April 11, 2024, DOT OIG announced Roderick Billingslea (Georgia) pleaded guilty to wire fraud and falsification of records after "reincarnating" multiple motor carriers (E-Cargo, Hidden Valley Transportation, US Transport, Midwest Express, Dispatch USA) to evade an FMCSA cease-operations order. This is a real, named, adjudicated chameleon-carrier case — useful as a qualitative illustrative example and possibly as one hand-verified positive-match case study — but it is a single prosecuted case, not a labeled dataset, and would need to be independently researched (court records, FMCSA registration history for the named entities) to turn into even one adjudicated benchmark example.

**Net finding: no labeled chameleon-carrier dataset was identified in the documented search through 2026-08-18.** Every source above either describes a targeting methodology without releasing validated matched-pair ground truth, or is an individual enforcement/adjudicative record requiring source-governed reconstruction. The FMCSA Report to Congress has since been retrieved and still publishes no precision, recall, false-positive rate, or adjudicated pair corpus. This supports a benchmark-construction gap; it does not prove that no qualifying artifact exists.

## Superseded benchmark construction recommendation

The semi-synthetic recommendation below is retained as dated scan history but is superseded by
[[e1-benchmark-sampling-and-split-plan]], [[dataset-e1-adjudicated-carrier-identity-cases]] and
[[e1-experiment-brief-and-readiness-map]]. It must not control implementation. In particular,
distinct seed or USDOT records are not negative legal-person labels, USDOT continuity is not Task
A gold, and synthetic corruption belongs only in development/challenge fixtures rather than the
representative entity-centric reference benchmark.

The original scan proposed a semi-synthetic benchmark seeded from real FMCSA records and an
adapted corruption engine. The accepted design now uses a probability-based, entity-centric real
reference cohort with independent adjudication and cluster-closure accounting. Synthetic
corruption remains useful for source-adapter, robustness and failure-mechanism tests, but it
cannot manufacture legal-person truth, authoritative negative pairs, reincarnation findings or
population prevalence.

This plan does not resolve a quantitative chameleon-carrier detection benchmark. Phase I may use a
small number of authoritative final dispositions as nonrepresentative Task C/disposition challenge
cases, but synthetic corruption is limited to observation noise and must not manufacture
reincarnation ground truth. E1 therefore makes no prevalence or detection-rate claim for
chameleon carriers. Any step requiring OpenCorporates paid/bulk access, state-registry cross-
referencing at scale, or additional authoritative disposition retrieval still requires budget,
authorization, or manual research and is not secured by this scan.
