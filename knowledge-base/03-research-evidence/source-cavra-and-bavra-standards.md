## Retrieval status — read this before citing anything below

**The CAVRA document itself was not retrieved.** The publisher's site offers a "concise
version" behind an email-capture form with Terms and Conditions, and states that the full
54-page document is available only by emailing `cassandra@logisticsriskexpert.com`. No
un-gated PDF was found in this scan. Everything below about CAVRA's *contents* is
therefore either the publisher's own description of its product (`vendor`) or trade-press
characterization (`secondary`) — **not** a reading of the standard. The same applies to
BAVRA.

`verification: snippet-only` applies to all content claims. `verification: confirmed`
applies only to: the existence of the two documents, their publisher, their gating, and
the FreightWaves release reporting.

## What the source establishes

**Authorship and commercial position.** CAVRA and BAVRA are published by Gaines Consulting
Group LLC. Cassandra Gaines is founder and CEO of **Carrier Assure**, a commercial carrier
vetting platform. Carrier Assure appears on this programme's competitor list (see G4 in
[[evidence]]). CAVRA is therefore a document authored by a market participant and marketed
as an industry standard; it is not the product of a consensus standards body, and it
carries no accreditation. FreightWaves reports it "was intentionally released as a public
industry resource rather than proprietary product" — that is a characterization of intent,
not of governance.

**Scope as described by the publisher and trade press** (`vendor`/`secondary`):

| Attribute | Stated value | Source of statement |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 54 pages (full); a shorter "concise version" is the form-gated download | publisher site |
| Structure | Four named principles: assessment, verification, risk, accountability | publisher + FreightWaves |
| Also described as | "nine core principles for carrier vetting" | FreightWaves |
| Topics covered | safety data, roadside inspection history, fraud indicators, identity verification, double-brokering risk, chameleon-carrier risk, shipment suitability, operational red flags, documentation, escalation, written vetting policies | publisher + FreightWaves |
| Release | 2026-06-18, via a webinar reported at 800+ attendees | FreightWaves |
| Uptake | "Downloaded by nearly 1,500 transportation professionals" | publisher site (self-reported, undated, no denominator) |
| Access | email-capture form; full version by email request | publisher site |

Note the internal inconsistency: the framework is described both as having **four**
principles (the acronym) and **nine core principles**. Both descriptions come from
reporting on the same release. This is not adjudicable without the document.

**Evidentiary bar.** The one substantive statement of CAVRA's evidentiary standard found
in this scan is Gaines' own formulation, quoted by FreightWaves:

> "The question is not whether every decision was perfect. The question is whether the
> decision was reasonable based on the information available at the time."

That is a reasonableness-at-time-of-decision standard, not a data-completeness or
verification-assurance standard. **No list of required data fields was retrievable.** Any
vault claim about "what fields CAVRA requires" is currently unsupportable.

**BAVRA.** 34 pages, released August 2026, addressed to shippers selecting and monitoring
brokers, forwarders, managed transportation providers, and motor carriers with brokerage
authority. FreightWaves reports the two frameworks are "intentionally connected" —
shippers are told to evaluate whether a prospective broker maintains a carrier-qualification
programme "aligned with CAVRA or equivalent standards." That is a self-referential
adoption mechanism worth noting when positioning against CAVRA.

## Negative finding — ASTM incorporation

**Confirmed absence, within the stated scope.** No connection between CAVRA and ASTM,
ASTM Committee F49, or any ASTM work item was found. Searched 2026-08-07:

- Web search: `"ASTM F49 CAVRA carrier vetting standard work item 2026"`;
  `"Cassandra Gaines ASTM standard carrier selection incorporate CAVRA"`;
  `"ASTM" "F49" carrier vetting OR "carrier selection" OR "broker" negligent selection standard`.
- The publisher's own two pages (`/cavra-standard/`, `/vetting-standards/`): no mention of
  ASTM or any standards body; no endorsement claimed.
- Both FreightWaves release articles: no mention of ASTM.
- ASTM's own F49 committee brochure, April 2025, listing every current work item — see
  [[source-astm-f49-committee]]. No carrier-vetting or carrier-selection item exists.

**Scope limit on this absence:** the ASTM brochure is dated April 2025 and therefore
predates CAVRA's June 2026 release. It rules out a *pre-existing* F49 carrier-vetting work
item. It cannot rule out a work item registered between April 2025 and today, because
`astm.org` returns HTTP 403 to automated retrieval and no current work-item list could be
obtained. The correct statement for the vault is: **no public evidence of ASTM
incorporation exists; a work-item ballot after April 2025 has not been ruled out and would
require either a manual visit to astm.org or direct contact with F49 leadership.**

This supports the existing correction in [[evidence]] §G5 — the secondhand team note that
"ASTM F49 leadership reached out to Gaines" remains unverified and must not be repeated as
fact.

## Limits and scope

- The 54-page and 34-page documents were not read. No claim about their internal
  requirements may be cited as load-bearing.
- "Nearly 1,500 downloads" is a self-reported vendor figure with no date, no denominator,
  and no independent confirmation. It is not evidence of industry adoption. It is not
  evidence that any broker, shipper, or carrier has *implemented* CAVRA.
- No adopting organization, no association endorsement, and no court citation of CAVRA was
  found in this scan.
- Trade-press articles are `secondary`. FreightWaves is the only outlet from which article
  text was retrieved; Logistics Management returned HTTP 403.

## Consequence for the programme

CAVRA is a **vendor-authored framework**, not a neutral standard. On the fold-in /
coordinate / position-against question this scan supports: do not fold CAVRA in as an
authority, and do not describe it in reviewer-facing prose as an industry or ASTM standard.
It may be cited as evidence that *the market perceives an unmet need for a documented
carrier-selection evidentiary standard* following the May 2026 SCOTUS decision — which is
an argument for this programme, not against it.
