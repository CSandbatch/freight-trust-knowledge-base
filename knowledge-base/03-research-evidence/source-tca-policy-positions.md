# Source — Truckload Carriers Association, Issues & Policies

## Citation

Truckload Carriers Association. *Issues & Policies*. <https://truckload.org/policy-positions/>
(retrieved 2026-08-07, HTTP 200). Advocacy landing page: <https://truckload.org/advocacy/>
(retrieved 2026-08-07, HTTP 200).

Secondary, for the detention position only: FreightWaves, "What lawmakers intend to do
about driver detention," **2020-06-04**,
<https://www.freightwaves.com/news/lawmakers-prod-fmcsa-to-move-on-driver-detention>

## What the source establishes

### Cargo theft — a published TCA policy position (`primary`)

TCA's own policy document states: "Losses from pilferage, theft, and hijacking of cargo
shipped by truck in interstate commerce continue to be substantial."

The position's distinctive feature is a **liability framing**: "Motor carriers are the
victims and not the perpetrators of cargo and vehicle theft." TCA calls for law-enforcement
investigation and prosecution rather than penalties directed at carriers.

TCA also lists a member resource titled "How to Report Freight Fraud" among its legislative
materials — evidence that freight fraud is within its active advocacy programme, though the
resource itself carries no policy language.

### Detention — a position exists but it is six years old (`secondary`)

TCA's *published* Issues & Policies page contains **no detention position**. The only TCA
detention position found in this scan comes from 2020 trade coverage of the INVEST in
America Act, quoting TCA's submission to FMCSA:

> "Because examples of detention can be quickly and easily identified through ELD data,
> FMCSA must determine its authority to act if these data are provided to the Agency by
> carriers."

and urging FMCSA "to explore its authority … to determine whether enforcement against
shippers which consistently force drivers to wait for several hours in detention is a legal
remedy for the detention time issue."

**This is the most directly relevant association statement found for Aim 2** — a carrier
association asserting that detention is *measurable from ELD data* and that the measurement
should drive enforcement. But it is `secondary`, it is dated **2020-06-04**, and TCA has not
carried it forward onto its current policy page. Cite it as *"TCA's position as stated to
FMCSA in 2020"* and never as TCA's current position. Re-verification requires locating
TCA's actual FMCSA docket comment.

The same 2020 article records, for contrast: ATA and the International Warehouse Logistics
Association "favor market-based solutions through commercial agreements rather than
regulatory intervention" on detention; OOIDA President Todd Spencer said members believe
brokers and shippers will not honour detention pay "unless there was some form of
motivation or incentive, whether it is through a federally mandated fine." All three are
2020 positions reported by a third party.

## Negative findings

**Not found on TCA's own published policy pages, as of 2026-08-07:** any position on
double brokering, carrier vetting or identity verification, broker transparency (49 CFR
371), or detention/dwell measurement. The policy document addresses cargo theft and general
economic-regulation principles; the other topics do not appear.

**Search scope:** truckload.org/policy-positions/, truckload.org/advocacy/ (both retrieved);
web searches `Truckload Carriers Association TCA position freight fraud cargo theft
detention time comments FMCSA` and `truckload.org TCA advocacy "detention" OR "driver
detention" position statement 2026`.

**Correction to the vault.** [[evidence]] §G6 records TCA as backing CORCA with President
Jim Mullen quoted and a "31% YoY rise in Q1 2026 deceptive pickup fraud" figure, sourced to
truckload.org and thetrucker.com. **That claim was not reproduced by this scan** — the CORCA
backing does not appear on TCA's own Issues & Policies page, and the 31% figure was
encountered only in third-party commentary with no stated population or method. Reported as
a possible over-attribution, not corrected here (scope boundary). TCA is also **not** a
signatory to the April 2026 DOJ cargo-theft coalition letter (see
[[source-doj-cargo-theft-coalition-letter]]).

## Limits and scope

- The Issues & Policies page was retrieved but its full policy text may be distributed
  across linked fact sheets that were not individually fetched. The absence findings above
  are scoped to the main policy page and the advocacy landing page.
- The 2020 detention quotes are trade-press reproductions of a regulatory comment, not the
  comment itself. `secondary`.
- The "31% deceptive pickup" figure has no confirmed source, population, or period and must
  not be cited.
