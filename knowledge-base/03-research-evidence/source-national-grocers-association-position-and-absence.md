## What the source establishes

### NGA has a real, dated, named position — on organized retail crime (`primary`)

On **2026-05-13**, NGA publicly applauded House passage of the **Combating Organized Retail
Crime Act (H.R. 2853)**, describing it as bipartisan legislation addressing organized retail
theft affecting independent grocers nationwide. This is a first-degree, attributable
association position with a date and a named officer.

Attributed to **Stephanie Johnson, NGA Senior Vice President and Head of Government Affairs**.
The portion confirmed verbatim from NGA's own page:

> "Organized retail crime is not a victimless crime. It threatens employees, raises…"

The remainder of the sentence — "…costs for consumers, disrupts local businesses, and puts
additional pressure on independent grocers who navigate success on razor-thin margins" — was
encountered only in search-index reproduction of trade coverage. It is `snippet-only` and must
be quoted, if at all, as reported by trade press rather than as read from NGA.

NGA's page notes the legislation "establishes coordination mechanisms to combat 'organized theft
rings'" and creates an "organized retail and supply chain crime coordination center," and
references "rapidly rising costs associated with organized theft, store security, and loss
prevention."

**This corroborates the same legislative vehicle NRF backs** — see
[[source-nrf-organized-retail-crime-and-cargo-theft]] — and independently confirms, from a
second association, that H.R. 2853 passed the House by 2026-05-13. FMI – The Food Industry
Association issued a parallel statement of support the same day (reported; not independently
retrieved).

### NGA's advocacy remit does not include transportation (`primary`)

NGA's own Advocacy page lists **three** issue areas, verbatim:

1. "Antitrust Laws"
2. "Food & Nutrition"
3. "Business Issues"

There is no transportation, freight, trucking, or supply-chain issue area. NGA's policy identity
is competition policy (its long-running concern with buyer power and price discrimination
against independent grocers), nutrition programme access, and small-business regulatory burden.

## Limits and scope

- **NGA's position is about theft from stores, not about freight.** The CORCA statement is scoped
  to organized retail theft and its cost to independent grocers. The word "supply chain" appears
  only as part of the *name* of the coordination centre the bill would create. There is no NGA
  statement about cargo in transit, carriers, brokers, or pickup.
- **Not a signatory to the April 28, 2026 DOJ cargo-theft coalition letter.** The full 24-entity
  signatory list was read directly (see
  [[source-nrf-organized-retail-crime-and-cargo-theft]]); NGA does not appear. NRF, RILA, and
  the U.S. Chamber do. This is a meaningful distinction: NGA supports the *legislation* but is
  not part of the cargo-theft *enforcement coalition*.
- The Johnson quotation is partially truncated in the retrieved rendering, as recorded above.
  Do not present the full sentence as read from NGA.
- CORCA's status is time-sensitive: House passage on or before 2026-05-13 is established; Senate
  disposition is not established by anything in this scan. `review_by` set accordingly.

## Negative findings — confirmed absences

**NGA holds no position on carrier identity verification, freight fraud, double brokering,
broker transparency (49 CFR 371), or detention/dwell measurement.**

Search scope, 2026-08-08: `nationalgrocers.org` Advocacy page and the CORCA news release (both
retrieved, HTTP 200); web searches `"National Grocers Association" cargo theft organized retail
crime CORCA position advocacy` and `"National Grocers Association" trucking freight detention
supply chain policy priorities 2026`. The second search surfaced no NGA material at all — its
results were the National Freight Strategic Plan, GCCA's cold-chain priorities, and generic
grocery-supply-chain commentary. No NGA comment filing, testimony, position paper, or press
release on any freight topic surfaced.

This is a **confirmed absence, not a failure to find**: NGA publishes its advocacy taxonomy,
that taxonomy has three branches, and none of them is transportation.

## Consequence for the programme

**NGA should be removed from the stakeholder matrix as a position-holder on any freight-identity
or detention question.** It is a competition-policy and food-policy body whose members are
independent grocers; its one adjacent position is on retail theft, and it reaches that subject
through the store, not the dock.

It may retain value as an **outreach channel to independent grocery receivers** — small,
numerous facilities whose dwell behaviour is exactly the long tail Aim 2 would struggle to
observe, and whose operators are structurally similar to the small carriers in
[[experiment-e4-participation-and-small-carrier-equity]]. That is an outreach hypothesis and a
team decision, not a documented position. Do not present NGA as an interested party.

**Pattern note across G6.** NGA is the third body in this set — with NAIT and, in advocacy
terms, FSA — whose apparent relevance dissolves on inspection. Three of thirteen bodies were on
the list by plausibility rather than by position. That is the finding G6 was designed to
produce.
