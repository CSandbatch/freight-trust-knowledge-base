# Source — National Retail Federation: cargo theft as organized retail crime

## Citation

1. **Congressional testimony (primary, read in full).** National Retail Federation, *Written
   Statement of the National Retail Federation*, submitted to the U.S. House Committee on the
   Judiciary, Subcommittee on Crime and Federal Government Surveillance, for the hearing
   "Protecting Consumers and Businesses: Confronting Organized Retail Crime," **December 17,
   2025**. 16 pp. including annexes. Retrieved 2026-08-08 from
   <https://www.congress.gov/119/meeting/house/118765/documents/HHRG-119-JU08-20251217-SD004.pdf>
   (HTTP 200; PDF read directly).
2. **Coalition letter (primary, read in full).** Multi-association letter to Acting Attorney
   General Todd Blanche, U.S. Department of Justice, **April 28, 2026**, with the FY2026 CJS
   appropriations report language enclosed. Retrieved 2026-08-08 from
   <https://www.trucking.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/DOJ%20Letter%20-%20Cargo%20Theft%20Appropriations%20Implementation%20-%20FINAL.pdf>
   (HTTP 200; PDF read directly, 5 pp.). Hosted by ATA; NRF is a signatory.
3. **Policy page (primary).** NRF, *Organized Retail Crime*.
   <https://nrf.com/advocacy/policy-issues/organized-retail-crime> (retrieved 2026-08-08,
   HTTP 200).
4. **Industry sign-on letter (primary, within item 1's annex).** NRF letter to congressional
   leadership supporting CORCA, **July 15, 2025**, with ~400 company signatories.

## What the source establishes

### NRF treats cargo theft as a component of organized retail crime, and has said so under its own name to Congress (`primary`)

NRF's definition of ORC, verbatim from its testimony: "theft or fraud activities conducted with
the intent to convert illegally obtained merchandise, cargo, cash or cash equivalents into
personal financial gain. It also must involve theft or fraud of multiple quantities, conducted
in concert by two or more people."

**Cargo is inside the definition.** NRF states: "Increasingly, organized criminal groups are
stealing cargo from warehouses, railcars and trucks, posing a direct threat to our supply
chain, commerce, the retail industry and our transportation partners. These thefts are not
isolated incidents by opportunistic criminals but part of a broader network of ORC where stolen
goods are resold to fund other criminal enterprises."

**The single most on-point sentence in the whole G6 set, verbatim:**

> "Many use cybercriminals or cybercrime tactics to pose as reputable brokers and distributors,
> then pick up trailers and re-route the cargo to criminal enterprises ready to resell and
> re-distribute goods both domestically and internationally."

This is a national shipper-side association stating, in congressional testimony, that
**broker/distributor impersonation followed by fraudulent trailer pickup is a recognised
mechanism** — not a vendor's marketing claim and not trade-press inference. NRF adds: "These
groups monitor shipping routes, identify security gaps and frequently conduct thefts in remote
areas where law enforcement response is limited."

### NRF's legislative ask is a federal coordination centre, not carrier verification (`primary`)

NRF "strongly supports" the **Combating Organized Retail Crime Act of 2025 (S.1404 / H.R.2853)**
and urges its passage. What the bill does, per NRF: it "enhances enforcement tools, modernizes
criminal statutes, and establishes a centralized coordination center" — an "Organized Retail and
Supply Chain Crime Coordination Center" — and would "establish and memorialize the public-private
partnerships necessary for information sharing among retailers, law enforcement and financial
institutions at the national level."

NRF also cites the **INFORM Consumers Act** (passed 2022, effective July 2023) as precedent it
supported, describing it verbatim as legislation that "requires online marketplaces to verify
the identity of individuals who sell a higher volume of goods." **NRF has therefore already
endorsed identity verification as a fraud remedy in an adjacent market** — that is a useful
precedent for Aim 1's political framing, and it is NRF's own characterisation.

### NRF's quantitative claims — population, period, method (`primary` for the reporting, survey for the method)

From NRF's *The Impact of Retail Theft & Violence 2025*, released end of October 2025, described
by NRF as "data provided by retailers themselves" — i.e. a **member survey**, self-reported,
not an administrative record:

| Figure | Population and period | Method |
|---|---|---|
| 50% of retailers reported increases in "cargo or supply chain thefts" tied to ORC networks | Surveyed retailers, reporting on 2025 | Survey, self-report |
| 18% increase in shoplifting incidents, 2023→2024 | Surveyed retailers | Survey, self-report |
| 17% rise in threats or acts of violence associated with those incidents, 2023→2024 | Surveyed retailers | Survey, self-report |
| 70% reported increases in phone scams; 55% in digital/ecommerce fraud | Surveyed retailers, 2025 | Survey, self-report |
| 67% reported involvement of a transnational organized theft group | Surveyed retailers | Survey, self-report |
| 51% say shoplifters exhibit more violence than a year earlier | Surveyed retailers | Survey, self-report |
| 91% say violence-related theft required increased employee training | Surveyed retailers | Survey, self-report |

The NRF policy page cites, from the same or a companion release, 66% reporting transnational ORC
involvement since 2024 and 90% agreeing federal action would help. **Note the 67%/66% and the
73%/51% pairs**: the July 2025 sign-on letter cites 73% saying shoplifters are more violent,
sourced to *The Impact of Retail Theft & Violence 2024* (December 17, 2024), while the December
2025 testimony cites 51% from the 2025 edition. These are **different survey years** and must
not be presented as a trend without saying so.

NRF's institutional scale claims: retail is "the nation's largest private-sector employer,
contributing $5.3 trillion to annual GDP and supporting one in four U.S. jobs — 55 million
working Americans." NRF's ORC-specific apparatus: a member-led **Loss Prevention Council**, the
**NRF PROTECT** annual conference (">2,000 retail executives, law enforcement and solution
providers"), and an **NRF ORC Investigators' Network**.

### NRF is a signatory to the April 2026 DOJ cargo-theft letter (`primary`, full text read)

The letter was addressed to Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche and urged DOJ to "fully
implement the funding provided by Congress in the FY2026 Commerce, Justice, Science, and Related
Agencies (CJS) Appropriations Act to establish dedicated special prosecutors focused on combating
supply chain fraud, cargo theft, organized retail crime (ORC), and related financial fraud
schemes, including gift card fraud."

Its diagnosis, verbatim: "Despite significant arrests by law enforcement, prosecutions frequently
do not follow. State and local prosecutors—already resource constrained—struggle to pursue
complex, multijurisdictional theft cases."

**The complete signatory list, verbatim and in order** (24 entities — recorded here in full so
that future absence claims about other bodies are checkable):

American Property Casualty Insurance Association · American Trucking Associations · Association
of American Railroads · Association of Home Appliance Manufacturers · Auctane · Catalyst
Brands · DHL · GE Appliances, Haier · Intermodal Association of North America · International
Bridge, Inc. · Meat Import Council of America · Milwaukee Tool · National Auto Auction
Association · National Independent Auto Dealers Association · National Milk Producers
Federation · **National Retail Federation** · Recycled Materials Association · Retail Industry
Leaders Association · Supply Chain Federation · The Travelers Companies, Inc. · U.S. Chamber of
Commerce · U.S. Dairy Export Council · UPS · World Shipping Council.

The enclosed FY2026 CJS report language (pp. 81–82) is itself a primary federal record: under
"Combatting Cargo Theft," the Committee "is concerned with the alarming rise in supply chain
fraud and cargo theft throughout the railroad, commercial motor vehicle, and intermodal
transportation systems," directs DOJ to brief within 120 days on "plans to establish regional
task forces, led by the FBI and in partnership with Homeland Security Investigations, the
Transportation Security Administration, the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration, and
Federal, State, and local enforcement agencies," and directs EOUSA to report within 90 days
identifying the U.S. Attorney's Offices "with the highest rates of crimes involving cargo theft,
including under sections 659, 1991, and 2117 of title 18, United States Code."

## Limits and scope

- **NRF's frame is criminal enforcement, not commercial verification.** Every remedy it asks for
  is prosecutorial or information-sharing: special prosecutors, a coordination centre, task
  forces, aggregation statutes. NRF does not ask for carrier vetting, identity standards, or
  registration reform. Do not represent NRF as supporting this programme's mechanism; represent
  it as documenting the harm and endorsing federated information-sharing.
- **All NRF percentages are self-reported retailer survey responses**, not measurements of theft.
  A respondent "reporting an increase" is not an increase. Population is NRF's survey frame,
  which is not published in the testimony.
- The "$5.3 trillion to annual GDP" figure is NRF's characterisation of retail's economic
  contribution and is not defined in the document; it is an association scale claim, not a
  measured contribution.
- CORCA's legislative status is time-sensitive. As of the December 2025 testimony it had "over
  180 co-sponsors in the House"; the NRF policy page says "over 200." Its House passage is
  recorded from a different source — see
  [[source-national-grocers-association-position-and-absence]]. `review_by` set accordingly.
- The July 15, 2025 signatory list of ~400 companies was read but is not reproduced here; it is
  a company sign-on, not an association position, and adds nothing to G6.

## Negative findings — confirmed absences

**NRF holds no position on driver detention, dwell measurement, or broker transparency (49 CFR
371).** Searched 2026-08-08: the full 16-page testimony (read directly — detention, dwell, and
49 CFR 371 do not appear), the DOJ letter (read directly — same), the NRF ORC policy page, plus
web search `"National Retail Federation" driver detention dwell time truck facility policy
broker transparency`. Results returned FreightWaves, TIA, and FMCSA material with no NRF
statement. Given that NRF members operate the receiving facilities where detention accrues, this
absence is itself worth noting: the party best placed to hold a position on facility dwell has
not taken one publicly.

## Consequence for the programme

NRF is the **strongest cargo-side citation available** for the mechanism Aim 1 targets, because
its testimony describes broker impersonation and fraudulent pickup in a body's own words, to
Congress, on the record. It is also a documented member of an active 24-entity coalition on
cargo theft — meaning the coalition itself, not just NRF, is a reachable audience. And its
endorsement of the INFORM Consumers Act establishes that NRF has already accepted
identity-verification-as-remedy in an adjacent market, which is the shortest available bridge
from NRF's enforcement frame to this programme's verification frame.
