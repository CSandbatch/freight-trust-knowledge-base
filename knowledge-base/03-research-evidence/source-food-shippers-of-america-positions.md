## What the source establishes

### What FSA is (`primary`)

A **501(c)(6) trade association** that "brings together a community of supply chain, logistics,
transportation, and warehousing professionals specifically focused on the food and beverage
business."

Its membership rule is the structurally important fact: **membership is limited to individuals
employed by companies that own or maintain physical possession of food products in the supply
chain** — food manufacturing, processing, grocery, restaurant, distribution, food service.
Service providers (transportation, warehouse, logistics, technology firms) are **excluded from
membership**, though they may attend the annual conference.

FSA is therefore a **beneficial-cargo-owner body**, not a carrier or intermediary body. Its
constituency is the *shipper and receiver* side — the same side that operates the facilities
whose event records Aim 2 proposes to reconstruct.

### FSA has published a cargo-theft position, and it names carrier identity verification (`primary`)

The October 2025 article is the strongest FSA statement found. Its definitions and its
recommendation are both directly on this programme's subject.

FSA's definition of strategic theft, verbatim: "planned, targeted theft of specific cargo,
sometimes involving load boards, fleet/identity impersonation, and/or double-brokering."

FSA names as current strategic cargo-theft trends: fictitious pickups, double-brokering,
identity theft, account takeovers, and fraudulent carriers.

**FSA's recommendation on carrier verification, verbatim** — the load-bearing sentence for
stakeholder-demand purposes: companies should "conduct thorough background checks, verify
insurance coverage, and confirm carrier identity before loading shipments. Using digital
verification tools can help detect fraudulent operators."

FSA's other recommendations: strengthen carrier partnerships, invest in GPS/geofencing, optimise
routing away from high-risk zones, train employees, and participate in industry crime-reporting
networks.

Figures FSA cites (all third-party, see limits): $35 billion annually in total supply-chain
cost from cargo theft (National Insurance Crime Bureau); $332 million stolen-cargo value in
2023 across 2,852 reported incidents (CargoNet, via ATRI); $116,397 average loss per incident;
41% of domestic thefts in California (2023).

### FSA has engaged on detention — but *ocean* detention, not truck-facility dwell (`primary`)

The February 2024 article records that FSA member companies pressed a federal regulator on
detention billing. Verbatim: food shippers including "Cargill, Ocean Spray Cranberries, Blue
Diamond Growers, Walmart, Target, and American Commodity Co." have "been part of the National
Shipper Advisory Committee (NSAC) that has been pushing FMC to expand oversight on demurrage
and terminal dwell fees."

The rule described is the Federal Maritime Commission's Ocean Shipping Reform Act
detention-and-demurrage billing rule, effective **2024-05-26** (except §541.6, pending further
data collection). Requirements FSA reports: invoices issued within 30 calendar days; invoices
may go only to the party contracting with the billing party *or* the consignee, not both and
not anyone else; the billed party has 30 days to request mitigation, refund, or waiver; the
billing party must attempt resolution within 30 days.

**This is a real, dated, documented instance of a shipper body demanding that dwell charges be
made attributable, timely, and contestable.** That is the same property Aim 2 asserts for
facility events. It is an analogy in the ocean/terminal mode, and the NSAC link connects to
[[source-astm-f49-committee]], which records an F49.01 work item on the "NSAC Recommended
Minimum Required Data Set."

### First-degree engagement on fraud is documented, at conference level (`secondary`)

FSA's 70th Annual Food Shippers Conference, **2026-03-01 to 2026-03-03**, Hilton Bonnet Creek
Resort, Orlando, drew "more than 1,300 food supply chain professionals." Sessions addressed
fraud, cargo theft, AI in fleet efficiency, and **carrier onboarding best practices**. FSA
President Darica Schneider (Senior Director of Transportation Operations, Univar Solutions) is
quoted on collaboration, not on fraud. FleetOwner reports that FSA experts emphasised "digital
tools for compliance and partner verification to reduce fraud and cargo theft risks" — a
paraphrase with no named speaker, so `secondary` and weak.

## Limits and scope

- The cargo-theft article is **educational guidance published by the association**, not a policy
  position adopted through a governance process. It is `primary` as evidence of what FSA tells
  its members; it is not an advocacy position and should not be cited as one.
- **All figures in it are third-party**, drawn from NICB, CargoNet, and ATRI across different
  years (2023 incident data; an undated $35B annual total). Populations and methods differ.
  They must not be combined, and they are not FSA findings.
- The detention/demurrage engagement is **ocean and terminal**, governed by FMC authority under
  OSRA. It is **not** a position on truck detention at shipper and receiver docks. The two are
  different modes, different regulators, and different populations. Do not present FSA as
  holding a position on driver detention.
- The named companies were on NSAC as companies, not as FSA delegates. FSA reports their
  participation; it does not claim to have led it.
- Both blog posts are attributed to "Staff," so no individual attribution is available.

## Negative findings — confirmed absences

**FSA publishes no policy positions and has no visible government-affairs function.** Searched
2026-08-08: `foodshippers.org` benefits page (retrieved), plus web searches
`"Food Shippers of America" cargo theft freight fraud double brokering position statement` and
`"foodshippers.org" blog detention dwell time carrier vetting fraud prevention`. No advocacy
page, no policy-positions page, no regulatory comment filing, no congressional testimony, and no
legislative endorsement surfaced. FSA's public apparatus is a conference, a blog, member
benefits, and scholarships.

**FSA is not a signatory to the April 28, 2026 DOJ cargo-theft coalition letter.** The full
signatory list was read directly — see
[[source-nrf-organized-retail-crime-and-cargo-theft]]. FSA does not appear on it.

**No FSA position on broker transparency (49 CFR 371) was found** in the same scan.

## Consequence for the programme

FSA belongs in the stakeholder matrix as a **beneficial-cargo-owner channel with published
demand-side language**, not as a policy ally. Its own guidance tells food shippers to "confirm
carrier identity before loading shipments" and that "digital verification tools can help" —
which is a documented statement of shipper-side demand for exactly the capability Aim 1
proposes, sourced to a shipper association rather than to a vendor. Because its membership
excludes service providers and consists of firms that operate the docks, it is also the most
plausible route to facility-side pilot participants for
[[experiment-e2-facility-event-provenance-and-dwell-reconstruction]] — an outreach hypothesis,
not a documented relationship.
