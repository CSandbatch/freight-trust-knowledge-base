# Samsara — vendor product documentation

**Classification.** Architecture: **closed proprietary data store with an open
*integration* layer (public API, partner marketplace); no knowledge-graph, ontology, or
semantic-interoperability claim.** Primary focus: **detention/visibility** (fleet
telematics, safety, and compliance).

## Citation

Samsara. "Connected Operations Platform." <https://www.samsara.com/products/platform/> —
retrieved 2026-08-07.

## What the source establishes, in its own terms

Product pillars as stated: telematics — **"Real-time GPS, routing, fuel, compliance"**;
video safety — **"AI dash cams, site security, in-cab alerts"**; asset tracking —
**"Diagnostics, maintenance, location tracking"**; and compliance/ELD, where
**"Intuitive tools and pre-built reports make it easy to manage regulations efficiently."**

Samsara does claim openness at the integration layer. Verbatim: **"350+ Integrations in
our global partner ecosystem"**, and **"Build faster and extend Samsara with consistent
APIs, developer tools, custom logic, and automated workflows."** The page uses the term
**"open platform"**.

## Axis-1 finding — the distinction matters here

Samsara is the case where axis 1 has to be read carefully rather than by keyword. It says
"open platform" and it means an extensibility surface: documented APIs and a partner
marketplace that let third parties build *on* Samsara. It does not claim a formal ontology,
a shared vocabulary, an ISO/W3C or industry data standard, or any mechanism for
independently governed parties to federate data without routing it through Samsara.

"Open API" and "interoperable architecture" are different properties, and the G4 argument
survives only if the vault holds that line. Samsara sits on the closed-proprietary side of
axis 1, with the integration-layer openness recorded rather than suppressed.

## Limits and scope

Vendor self-description; not independent validation. "350+ integrations" is a company-
stated count with no definition of what qualifies as an integration. Samsara's scope is
broader than freight — construction, field services, and public sector fleets — so it is
only partially comparable to the freight-specific entries in G4.
