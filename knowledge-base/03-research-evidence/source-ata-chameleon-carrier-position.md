# Source — American Trucking Associations, published position on chameleon carriers and registration fraud

## Citation

American Trucking Associations. "ATA Backs SAFE Act to Combat Chameleon Carriers,"
2026-02-13. <https://www.trucking.org/news-insights/ata-backs-safe-act-combat-chameleon-carriers>
(retrieved 2026-08-07, HTTP 200).

American Trucking Associations. "Stop the Shell Game on our Highways: Shut Down Chameleon
Carriers," 2026-04-14.
<https://www.trucking.org/news-insights/stop-shell-game-our-highways-shut-down-chameleon-carriers>
(retrieved 2026-08-07, HTTP 200).

Both are ATA's own publications on ATA's own domain. This **upgrades** the ATA row in
[[evidence]] §G6 from `secondary` (trade coverage) to `primary`.

## What the source establishes

**ATA has an explicit, dated, public position on carrier identity fraud.** It is not
inferred from remit.

**Definition ATA uses.** Chameleon carriers are operators who, in ATA's words, "rack up
serious safety violations, then dissolve and reappear under a new name" — a practice that
"puts the public at risk and punishes the vast majority of professional drivers."

**Quoted position, SAFE Act.** Alex Rosen, ATA Senior Vice President of Legislative
Affairs: "Chameleon carriers are a plague on our nation's highways, putting all motorists
at risk." ATA describes the bill as "commonsense policy" and states it will work toward
including the measure in the next Highway Bill.

**What ATA says the SAFE Act would require** — these four items are the closest thing found
in this scan to an association specification of a detection system, and they map directly
onto this programme's Aim 1:

1. A nationwide study of chameleon carriers' scope and impact.
2. Direct FMCSA to create **automated tools to flag suspicious registration applications**.
3. Strengthen federal–state coordination to identify red flags.
4. **Require human review and establish appeals processes.**

Item 2 is an automated-indicator requirement. Item 4 is an abstention-and-redress
requirement. Together they are a trade association endorsing exactly the
automated-flag-plus-human-review-plus-appeal structure this programme proposes in G13. That
is a usable alignment claim.

**ATA's four asks of Congress** (April 2026 post):

1. Strengthen registration systems via the SAFER Transport Act and the SAFE Act, so that
   operators shut down for safety failures cannot regain operating authority under new
   identities.
2. "Provide USDOT and FMCSA with the funding needed to hire and retain investigators,
   modernize systems, and carry out the agency's safety-critical mission."
3. End ELD self-certification; require mandatory third-party certification.
4. More robust in-person new-entrant audits rather than virtual audits.

**Enforcement-capacity figure.** ATA cites FMCSA Administrator Derek Barrs: "there are only
350 FMCSA investigators overseeing the 700,000+ motor carriers operating on America's
roads."

## Limits and scope

- The 350-investigator / 700,000-carrier figures are **ATA quoting an FMCSA official**, not
  an FMCSA publication. Attribute as *"ATA, quoting FMCSA Administrator Derek Barrs (April
  2026)"*. Do not cite as an FMCSA statistic without retrieving the FMCSA source. The
  population, date, and counting method for "700,000+" are not stated.
- The 2026-04-14 post has no named author. It is institutional ATA content.
- These are advocacy documents. They state ATA's policy preference; they are not evidence
  about the prevalence or detectability of chameleon carriers.
- **No ATA position on detention or dwell measurement was found in this scan.** A separate,
  older report (FreightWaves, 2020-06-04) states ATA favoured market-based solutions through
  commercial agreements rather than regulation on detention. That is `secondary` and six
  years old; do not treat it as ATA's current position without re-verification.
- ATA is also a signatory to the April 2026 DOJ cargo-theft coalition letter — see
  [[source-doj-cargo-theft-coalition-letter]].
