# FMCSA URSA — automated chameleon-risk screening as prior SBIR work

## Citation

Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration, *Utility for Risk-Based Screening and Assessment (URSA)*.

<https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/research-and-analysis/research/utility-risk-based-screening-and-assessment-ursa>

Page last updated 2024-05-30; retrieved 2026-08-08.

## What the source establishes

FMCSA states that an earlier **Small Business Innovation Research Phase I project**, concluded in August 2014, studied methods for identifying likely high-risk carriers and reincarnated carriers and defined an automated prototype for data analysis.

The subsequent URSA award produced an algorithm and software for automated risk assessment of the likelihood that an operating-authority applicant was a chameleon carrier, was attempting to reincarnate, or was otherwise attempting to obtain authority illicitly. FMCSA reports implementation of the URSA tool in the URS-1 process, with prototype implementation in September 2015 and integration into URS-1 in March 2016.

## Critical novelty constraint

This source invalidates any E1 novelty claim of the form:

- first automated chameleon-carrier screen;
- first risk-based carrier-application matching system;
- first FMCSA-adjacent use of algorithms to identify reincarnation;
- first SBIR project addressing automated chameleon-carrier screening.

E1 must be differentiated on **scientific target and evaluation architecture**, not on the existence of carrier-matching automation.

Potentially defensible differentiators, subject to evidence:

- a public/reusable adjudicated carrier identity and relationship benchmark;
- explicit separation of legal identity, registration continuity, affiliation, succession, substantial continuity, and regulatory disposition;
- provenance and valid-time/observation-time semantics;
- held-out evaluation with published precision/recall/calibration/abstention and subgroup error;
- contestability, evidence packets, and correction;
- model comparison against deterministic/probabilistic/graph-assisted baselines rather than production risk scoring alone.
