There was significant discussion during this week’s ASTM F49 terminology subcommittee call on related topics. A new member of the group was on the call from PTTR load board https://pttrloadboard.com/ , and the chair of the committee was quizzing him about his thoughts on duty of care related to selecting a carrier for a load.  I guess there was a recent US Supreme Court decision regarding responsibilities (and liability) of brokers that determined that they have a duty of care on selecting carriers, though it didn’t define what it was.  So there was a lot of discussion in the meeting about what data might be needed to support this duty of care.  This seemed to have a high overlap to the things (data) we had discussed in the load and carrier tracking OKN opportunity.

 

There was an article from Freight Waves on the decision.  The author of that article, Cassandra Gaines, has proposed her own “standard” related to the issue called “The CAVRA Standard” <https://www.logisticsriskexpert.com/cavra-standard/>. Some ASTM F49 committee leadership had previously reached out to Cassandra Gaines about using that as a starting point for developing a Guide in ASTM to help brokers, carriers, and shippers better navigate this, but have not gotten any response.

 

The PTTR Load Board contact noted that they are working on additional capabilities to collect and provide information that could be used for this (like MCDOT number for the carrier and VIN of the vehicle to be used).  Some of the same data used to verify the carrier could be used to detect or lower the risk of theft, and the load theft problem was also mentioned in our discussions.
